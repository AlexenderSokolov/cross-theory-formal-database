\begin{lemma}[Lifting divisibility from the localization]%
\label{lem:lift-dvd}
Let $S$ be prime-generated, $p$ irreducible, and suppose $p$ avoids~$S$.  If
$p/1\mid a/1$ in $S^{-1}R$, then $p\mid a$ in~$R$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
From the divisibility in the localization one obtains $s\in S$ and $c\in R$
with $s\cdot a = p\cdot c$.  Factor $s=q_1\cdots q_n$ into primes from~$S$.
Because $p$ avoids~$S$, we have $p\nmid q_i$ for each~$i$.  An auxiliary
result then peels off the prime factors one at a time: at each step, the
prime~$q_i$ divides $p\cdot(\text{something})$ but cannot divide~$p$, so by
primality of~$q_i$ it divides the cofactor.  After all factors are consumed,
$p\mid a$.
\end{proof}

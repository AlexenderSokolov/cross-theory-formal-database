2 Definitions and conventions
The unique closed topological surface of genus g for g ≥ 2 will be denoted by Sg . When Sg has the
additional structure of a marked hyperbolic structure, i.e., it represents a point of Teichmüller space
Tg of Sg , it will be denoted by Sg .
    Curves on Sg are nontrivial isotopy classes of embeddings of S 1 into Sg , where S 1 is the unpointed,
unoriented 1-sphere. A curve on Sg is assumed to inherit a marking, and by abuse of notation, the
word curve on Sg will often be confused with the image of its unique geodesic representative. The
term geodesic will always refer to a curve except in the case of a geodesic arc. The set of curves on
Sg will be denoted by Cg . A curve will usually be denoted by lowercase c with a subscript, while
uppercase C is reserved for finite sets of curves. A geodesic on Sg of smallest length will be called a
systole. The systole function fsys is the piecewise smooth function Tg → R+ whose value at a point
x is given by the length of the systoles. Throughout the paper, it will always be assumed that the
elements of a set C of curves have pairwise geometric intersection number at most one, mirroring a
property of sets of systoles.
    A set of curves on Sg fills the surface if the complement of a set of representatives in minimal
position consists of a set of topological disks. The Thurston spine Pg is therefore the set of all points
in Tg at which the systoles fill.
    In this paper, a cell is homeomorphic to an open ball. The standard definition of a cell in a CW
complex is closed; these will be understood to be the closures of the cells discussed in this paper. By
abuse of notation, vertices, edges and faces of a cell will be used to refer to the vertices, edges and
faces of the closure of the cell.
    The symbol ⊂ will be used to denote subset. For example, {1} ⊂ {1, 2} and {1, 2} ⊂ {1, 2},
whereas ⊊ is used to rule out the possibility that the sets are equal.
    Length functions. Length functions generalise the notion of the length of a curve. A curve c
on Sg determines an analytic function L(c) : Tg → R+ whose value at a point x is equal to the

    The sublevel set of L(c) is the set of all points of Tg for which L(c) is less than or equal the constant

Similarly, the superlevel set {x ∈ Tg | L(c)(x) ≥ l} will be denoted by L(c)≥l .
    Local finiteness. The lengths of elements of C is known to satisfy a property that will be referred
to as local finiteness. It can be proven from the collar lemma that for any x ∈ Tg , the set of curves
of length within ϵ of fsys (x) is finite. Consequently, there is a neighbourhood of x ∈ Tg in which the
systoles are contained in the set of curves that are realised as systoles at x.

Definition 2.1 (systole stratum). A systole stratum Sys(C) is the subset of Tg on which C is the
set of systoles.

    Here the term stratification is used in a minimal sense, following Thurston, to mean a disjoint
union of locally closed subsets, for which each point is on the boundary of only finitely many of these
locally closed subsets (strata).
    The existence of a triangulation of Tg compatible with the stratification follows from [12]. A
triangulation of this type is assumed when referring to the boundary of a stratum, or the tangent
vector or 1-sided limit of tangent vectors to strata of Pg .
    At first sight it might seem surprising that Pg is nonempty. This is a consequence of the following
proposition, and of the theorem due to Bers’ [4] that fsys is bounded from above.

Proposition 2.2 ([21, Proposition 0.1], [3, Lemma 4]). Let C be a set of curves on Sg that do not
fill. Then at any point x of Tg , there is an open cone of derivations in Tx Tg whose evaluation on
every element of {L(c) | c ∈ C} is strictly positive at x.

   The following is a short proof of Proposition 2.2 suggested by an anonymous referee.

Proof. Since C does not fill, there exists a curve c∗ disjoint from all the curves in C. Dehn twisting
around c∗ takes x to a different point x′ without changing the lengths of curves in C. Since length
functions are convex along Weil–Petersson geodesics [23], the tangent vector v at x to the Weil–
Petersson geodesic from x to x′ is a direction in which the lengths of all the curves in C are decreasing
to first order. It follows that v is contained in an open cone of vectors satisfying the proposition.

    Twist flow. In addition to a length function L(c), a curve c on Sg determines a flow R×Tg → Tg .
Choose any set of Fenchel–Nielsen coordinates on Tg defined using a set of curves containing c. Then
a point (r, x) is mapped to the point in Tg with Fenchel–Nielsen coordinates equal to the coordinates
at x, except for the twist parameter around c, which is incremented by r. A flowline will be called
a twist path and denoted by γc,x , where x = γc,x (0). A twist path is a special case of an earthquake
path, for which it is known that length functions are convex [10].
    This flow will be implicit in many of the arguments in this paper, for the simple reason that for
any c1 and c2 disjoint, c1 determines a flow that leaves the level sets of L(c2 ) invariant.

    Tangent cones and cones of increase. There are many places throughout this paper at which
objects similar to polyhedra are used. For a polyhedron P in Rd , the tangent cone at a point x ∈ ∂P
is the set of v ∈ Tx Rd such that v = γ̇(0) for a smooth oriented path γ(t) with γ(0) = x and γ(ϵ) in
P for suﬀiciently small ϵ > 0. For background and definitions relating to polyhedra, see [6] or [2].
    As Pg can be triangulated, the tangent cone of Pg at a point x can be defined analogously. When
x is on the boundary of more than one simplex of Pg , the tangent cone is the union of the tangent
cones of the simplices with x on the boundary. Tangent cones of strata are defined analogously. For
a triangulation compatible with the stratification, the tangent cone to Sys(C) at x is the union of
the tangent cones of the simplices with interior contained in Sys(C) with x on the boundary.
    Since fsys is only piecewise smooth, it does not have a gradient at every point. The level sets of
fsys are a locally finite intersection of level sets of curves in Tg . On a neighbourhood of x ∈ Tg at
which C is the set of systoles, by local finiteness the level set of fsys ∩ passing through x lies on the
boundary of the intersection I(C, x) defined as follows I(C, x) := c∈C L(c)≥fsys (x) . Each of these
sublevel sets is linear with respect to some set of Fenchel–Nielsen coordinates, but as the systoles can
intersect, I(C, x) is not always an intersection of half-spaces linear with respect to the same set of
coordinates. Nevertheless, it is possible to define a tangent cone in the same way as for a polyhedron,
and the cone of increase of fsys at a point x is defined to be the tangent cone of I(C, x).
    The cone of increase of fsys will be called full if it has dimension equal to that of Tg , namely 6g − 6.
Since fsys is only a topological, but not smooth, Morse function, it is possible that there exist regular
points at which the cone of increase of fsys is not full. If x is one such point, then fsys can increase
away from x to at most second order. Following Schmutz, these points will be called boundary points
of fsys . It was shown in [19] that the set of critical and boundary points of fsys are a discrete subset
of Pg . An alternative definition will be given in Section 2.1, and these points will be studied in
Section 5.
    Wherever it is nonempty, ∂I(C, x) is a piecewise smooth, codimension 1 submanifold of Tg , and
as such, it makes sense to speak of y 6= x on ∂I(C, x). A point y on ∂I(C, x) will be called a vertex
of I(C, x) if the tangent cone to I(C, x) at y contains no vector subspaces of Ty Tg . Otherwise, y will
be said to be on a face, where the face corresponds to a maximal vector subspace of the tangent cone
of I(C, x) at y, and two such subspaces at points y1 and y2 determine the same face if y1 and y2
are connected by a path in ∂I(C, x) along which the vector subspace has fixed dimension and varies
smoothly. When the face has codimension 1 in Tg , it will be∩called a facet.
    When I(C, x) is replaced by the intersection D(C, t) := c∈C L(c)≤t tangent cones, facets, faces
and vertices are defined analogously.
    The fact that fsys is a topological Morse function [1] implies that the set of unit tangent vectors
in the cone of increase at a regular point of fsys is a topological ball [15]. Removing the zero vector
from the cone of increase does not give a disconnected set.

    Thurston’s Lipschitz map construction. A construction of a map Tg → Tg will now be
surveyed. This is explained in detail in [16]. The input of this construction is (m, A), where m is
a geodesic multicurve on Sg and A is a set of pairwise disjoint geodesic arcs with endpoints on m,
meeting m at right angles, and with the property that A has nonzero geometric intersection number
with every boundary component of a tubular neighbourhood of m. The output of the construction
is a family of maps Tg → Tg which are used to control the change in the lengths of some curves
relative to others. This construction was used in [21] to prove Proposition 2.2, with m isotopic to
the boundary of the subsurface filled by C, and A a set of arcs that intersect every curve in C.
    Start with a surface Sg representing a point of Tg . Cut Sg along the geodesic representative
of m to get a union of hyperbolic surfaces with boundary. On every boundary component of each
of these surfaces, attach a hyperbolic annulus, to obtain a surface without boundary, as shown in
Figure 1. Each of the geodesic representatives of arcs in A can be extended to a bi-infinite geodesic
in these extended surfaces. Cut the extended surfaces along each bi-infinite geodesic, and glue in a
strip of the hyperbolic plane bounded by two infinite geodesics. The widths of the geodesic strips
is chosen in such a way that for every curve c in m, the curves cL and cR in the extended surfaces
corresponding to c have the same length after the strips have been glued in. For each curve c in
m, cut the extended surfaces along the geodesic representatives of cL and cR , and glue the surface
together along the resulting boundary components. This gives a new hyperbolic structure. Clearly
there are many choices involved in this construction.
    In this paper, as in [21], limits are taken as the widths of the strips are allowed to approach zero.
The resulting 1-parameter families of maps do not change the length of any curve disjoint from the

order with the parameter, the tangent vectors to these 1-parameter families lengthen to first order
every curve in m and every curve with geodesic representative disjoint from m that intersects one or
more of the arcs in A.

2.1 Sets of minima
As an attempt at parameterising Tg without reference to punctures or marked points, Schmutz
introduced the concept of cell decompositions of Teichmüller space parametrised by length functions.
Schmutz’s construction will be surveyed here as it provides insight into the differential topology of
Tg .

     A length function L(A, C) with fixed A ∈ R+ is convex with respect to the Weil–Petersson metric
[23]. A necessary and suﬀicient condition for x to represent the minimum of L(A, C) in Tg is therefore
that the gradient of L(A, C) is zero at x. When C is eutactic at x, there exists an A for which the
unique minimum of L(A, C) occurs at x. If C is not a filling set, Proposition 2.2 ensures that L(A, C)
has no minimum in the interior of Tg .
Definition 2.3 (Min(C)). The subset Min(C) of Tg is the set of all points at which L(A, C) has a
minimum for some A with strictly positive entries. Alternatively, Min(C) is the set of all x ∈ Tg at
which C is eutactic.
    In Section 3, minima of sets of curves will be defined. This should not be confused with minima
of length functions in the definition of Min(C).
    There is a notion of closure of Min(C) that will be denoted by Min(C).

that fills Sg . A point x of Tg is in Min(C) if and only if there does not exist a derivation in Tx Tg

at which Sys(C) intersects Min(C ′ ), for C ′ ⊊ C. There are reasons for calling such points boundary
points of fsys , but boundary points of fsys should not be confused with the usual topological notion
of boundary. The structure of critical and boundary points of fsys will be discussed in Section 5. By
[19, Corollary 22, Theorem 23], critical points and boundary points of fsys are isolated, so there are
only finitely many of them modulo the action of the mapping class group.

3 Minimal filling sets of curves
Suppose C is a filling set of curves on Sg , any two of which intersect in at most one point. The set
C will be called a minimal filling set of curves if no proper subset of C fills. This section begins
by proving some key lemmas about minimal filling sets of curves, and ends by discussing a property
that generalises many of the important properties of minimal filling sets of curves.
   A basic property of minimal filling sets of curves is the following, which will be assumed from
now on without comment.

Lemma 3.1. Suppose C is a minimal filling set of curves. For any curve c ∈ C, there is a curve c∗
that intersects c but does not intersect any of the other curves in C.

Proof. Suppose every curve on Sg that intersects c ∈ C also intersects a curve in C \{c}. Then C \ {c}
fills, contradicting the assumption that C is a minimal filling set.

    All known examples of locally top-dimensional strata of Pg have systoles that are minimal filling
sets. The diﬀiculty in proving this is always the case is due to a very specific configuration of linearly
dependent length functions discussed in Remark 4.7. It will be shown that loci of minimal filling sets
of curves have beautiful properties that generalise to locally top-dimensional strata of Pg .

3.1 Key lemmas
This subsection proves some key lemmas about minimal filling sets of curves and their loci, which
will now be defined.

Definition 3.2 (locus E(C) labelled by C). The locus of points in Tg at which the lengths of all
curves in C are equal will be denoted by E(C), and called the locus labelled by C. Alternatively,
E(C) = {x ∈ Tg | L(c)(x) = L(c′ )(x) ∀c, c′ ∈ C}.

    Note that Sys(C) ⊂ E(C).
    The set E(C) is called a locus to emphasise the similarity with the locus of points equidistant from
a finite set of points {p1 , . . . , pk } in Rn . The analogy is drawn by replacing the set of points in Rn by
points in the completion of Tg with respect to the Weil–Petersson metric, where pi for i = 1, . . . , k
is a point at which the curve ci ∈ C has been pinched. Note that by convexity, L(ci ) increases
monotonically along a Weil–Petersson geodesic emerging from pi .
    Recall that C is the set of curves on Sg . Denote by F (C) the map from Tg to RC taking x to the
collection of lengths {L(c)(x) | c ∈ C}, and by F (C) : Tg → RC /R the composition of this map with
the projection from RC (arbitrary maps from C to R) to its quotient by R (the constant maps). Fix
C and d ∈ RC /R. Then the locus E(C, d) is defined to be the set of points in Tg for which the lengths
of the curves in C realise the specified differences in d|C , in other words

                     E(C, d) = {x ∈ Tg | L(c)(x) + d|c = L(c′ )(x) + d|c′ ∀c, c′ ∈ C}.                  (3.1)

   When every element of d|C is zero, d will be omitted.
   The next lemma will be used in proving results about loci of minimal filling sets of curves.

Lemma 3.3. For a filling set C of curves, suppose C \ {c} is not filling for some c ∈ C. Then for
any x ∈ Tg , there exists a derivation v ∈ Tx Tg such that vL(c)(x) < 0 and vL(c′ )(x) > 0 for all
c′ ∈ C \ {c}.

Definition 3.4 (C-generic). Suppose C is a (not necessarily proper) subset of a minimal filling set
of curves. A point x ∈ Tg is C-generic if for every c ∈ C, the sum of the cosines of the angles of
intersection of c with c∗ is nonzero, for some choice of c∗ . Alternatively, the length function L(c) is
strictly convex along the twist path γc∗ ,x and hence has a unique minimum. The point x is C-generic
if for every c ∈ C, x is not the unique minimum of L(c) on γc∗ ,x .

3.2 Balanced loci
This subsection discusses a property of loci. This concept is related to the way loci of filling sets
of curves intersect corresponding sets of minima, leading to a trichotomy of three fundamentally
different types of loci.
    Suppose now that C is any finite set of curves on Sg that intersect pairwise at most once. It follows
from Lojasiewicz’s theorem, [12] that E(C, d) admits a triangulation, i.e., it is a simplicial complex.
Consequently, E(C, d) from equation (3.1) has a dimension, and in the interior of each simplex, it
has a tangent space. In what follows, vC will always denote a vector or vector field that specifies
a direction in which the lengths of the curves in C are all increasing at the same rate. Whenever
possible, vC is chosen to be contained in or on the boundary of the convex hull of {∇L(c) | c ∈ C}.

Definition 3.11 (E(C, d) is balanced, semi-balanced or unbalanced at x). Suppose x is a point of
E(C, d). Then

   • E(C, d) is balanced at x if x is in Min(C) or there is a nonzero vector vC in Tx E(C, d) in
     the interior of the convex hull of {∇L(c)(x) | c ∈ C}. Equivalently, if V is the orthogonal
     complement of vC (x), C is V -eutactic.
   • E(C, d) is semi-balanced at x if it is not balanced but x is in Min(C ′ ) for C ′ ⊊ C or there is a
     nonzero vector vC on the boundary of the convex hull of {∇L(c)(x) | c ∈ C}.
   • E(C, d) is unbalanced at x if there exists a nonzero vector vC at x and E(C, d) is neither
     balanced nor semi-balanced.

4 Proof of the Morse–Smale properties
The purpose of this section and the next is to show that Pg contains a choice of unstable manifolds
of the critical points of fsys . This section studies the behaviour of Pg away from critical points and
boundary points of fsys , i.e., at points at which the cone of increase is full. It will be shown that the
locally top-dimensional strata making up the cells of Pg are balanced, and that restricting fsys to Pg
does not create critical points. In this way, the intuition of Thurston that fsys is increasing towards
Pg is encoded.
     The main results of this section will be proven by generalising the properties of strata of minimal
filling sets to the locally top-dimensional strata of Pg . The simple case is described in the next
lemma.

Lemma 4.1. Suppose x ∈ Sys(C) and the set of vectors {∇L(c)(x) | c ∈ C} is linearly independent.
Then Sys(C) is on the boundary of Sys(C ′ ) for every nonempty C ′ ⊊ C.

Proof. For any fixed C ′ ⊂ C, E(C) ⊂ E(C ′ ), so E(C ′ ) is not empty. The linear independence
assumption then ensures that the codimension of E(C ′ ) in Tg is equal to |C ′ | − 1, and that the
tangent space at x to the level set of E(C ′ ) passing through x is given by the orthogonal complement
of Span{∇L(c) | c ∈ C ′ }. Choose c1 ∈ C. It follows from local finiteness that the nonzero vector
obtained by projecting ∇L(c1 )(x) onto the orthogonal complement of Span{∇L(c) | c ∈ C \ {c1 }}

also to x, can be constructed similarly. This construction can be iterated to prove the lemma.

Theorem 4.6. Where a stratum of Pg is locally top-dimensional it is balanced. If x ∈ Pg is not
balanced, there are points directly above x in Pg that are balanced.
Proof. The theorem is proven by assuming x is in a locally top-dimensional stratum Sys(C) of Pg
and using Proposition 3.20 to derive a contradiction when Sys(C) is not balanced at x.
     Suppose x ∈ Sys(C) is in a locally top-dimensional stratum in Pg that is not balanced at x. It
follows that x is not a critical point of fsys . If x were a boundary point of fsys , as discussed in Section
5, these are known to have higher-dimensional balanced strata in Pg above them. It is therefore
possible to assume that the cone of increase at x is full.
     On a neighbourhood of x, Sys(C) is not contained in a level set of fsys . If it were, Theorem 4.5
would give a stratum Sys(C ′ ) above x in Pg with C ′ ⊂ C and for which there exists a vC ′ in the
tangent cone to Sys(C ′ ) at points suﬀiciently close to x. This shows it is possible to construct a
stratum in Pg directly above x and with larger dimension than Sys(C), contradicting the assumption
that x is in a locally top-dimensional stratum.
     Denote by c(f ) the set of curves corresponding to the vertices of f , where f is the face of the convex
hull of {∇L(c)(x) | c ∈ C} from Proposition 3.20. The existence of a larger-dimensional stratum
above x ∈ Sys(C) is a consequence of Proposition 3.20 and the fact that every facet fi of the convex
hull of {∇L(c)(x) | c ∈ C} determines a stratum Sys(c′ (fi )) adjacent to x, where c′ (fi ) ⊂ c(fi )
fills the same subsurface of Sg as c(fi ). To prove this fact about facets, note that the span of
{∇L(c)(x) | c ∈ c(fi )} has a 1-dimensional orthogonal complement in Span{∇L(c)(x) | c ∈ C}.
Denote by ofi a tangent vector in Tx Tg to this orthogonal complement. The fact that fi is a facet
is used to ensure that there is a choice of ofi with strictly positive inner product with every element
of {∇L(c)(x) | c ∈ C \ c(fi )}. To first order, o(fi ) appears to be in the tangent cone at x to a stratum
Sys(c(fi )) adjacent to x. The construction in the proof of Theorem 4.5 then shows that ofi is in the
tangent cone at x to a stratum Sys(c′ (fi )), where c′ (fi ) ⊂ c(fi ) fills the same subsurface as c(fi ). The
stratum Sys(c′ (fi )) has higher dimension than Sys(C) at x, giving the required contradiction.
Remark 4.7. There are two obstacles in trying to prove that sets of systoles on locally top-
dimensional strata of Pg are all minimal filling sets. The first one is in ruling out accidental systoles.
The second is that the converse of Lemma 2.5 does not hold. Not every filling subset C ′ of C de-
termines a set of minima Min(C ′ ) contained in ∂Min(C). This could give rise to higher-dimensional
analogues of the following: Let C = {c1 , c2 , c3 , c4 } where {∇L(c)(x) | c ∈ C} are linearly dependent
and vC (x) is nonzero and in the convex hull of {∇L(c)(x) | c ∈ C}. Suppose also that {c1 , c2 }
is a minimal filling set, and c1 and c2 are the labels of a pair of vertices of the convex hull of
{∇L(c)(x) | c ∈ C} diagonally opposite each other. There seems to be no reason to expect that any
of the faces of the convex hull determine a set of filling systoles.

5 Critical points and boundary points of the systole function
The purpose of this section is to study the structure of Pg around critical and boundary points of
fsys . Section 7 later gives an example that is interesting in its own right, illustrating the theorem
proven in this section. The exposition begins with critical points.
     Recalling the definition of a topological Morse function, on a neighbourhood of a critical point p
of index j there is a homeomorphism ψ that fixes the critical point. This homeomorphism has the
property that there exists a smooth chart on a neighbourhood of p such that

                       fsys ◦ ψ(x) − fsys (p) = −x21 − · · · − x2j + x2j+1 + · · · + x2n .

In this sense, the level sets of fsys on a neighbourhood of a critical point of index j are the same, up
to homeomorphism, as for a critical point of index j of a smooth Morse function. It was shown in [1]
that critical points of fsys occur exactly where Sys(C) intersects Min(C). The index j is then equal
to the dimension of the span of {∇L(c) | c ∈ C} at the critical point.
    Suppose p is a critical point of index j in the intersection of Sys(C) with Min(C). It follows from
local finiteness that there is a neighbourhood of p in Tg on which the systoles are contained in the
set C. Convexity of length functions implies that the subspace {∇L(c) | c ∈ C}⊥ is contained in
the cone of increase of fsys at p. Within Min(C), in every direction there is by definition a curve or
curves in C whose length is decreasing away from p to first order. For a point q in Min(C) near p,
the systoles are the subset of curves in C whose lengths are decreasing fastest away from p in the
direction of q. It follows that j ≤ |C| − 1 and on a neighbourhood of p, the level set of fsys containing
p intersects Min(C) in the single point p. This is illustrated schematically in Figure 8 for the toy
example with two systoles.

from p.

Proof. By local finiteness, convexity of length functions and the definition of Min(C), the subspace

first order and all the directions in which fsys is increasing at p to second order are exactly given by
{∇L(c)(p) | c ∈ C}⊥ .

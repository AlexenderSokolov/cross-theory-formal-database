Definition 1.1 ([16, 18]). An analytic function F (t), where t = t1 , t2 , . . . , tn ∈ U ⊂ Cn defined in
an open subset of Cn , is considered a solution of the WDVV (Witten–Dijkgraaf–Verlinde–Verlinde)
                                              3
equation if its third derivatives cαβγ = ∂tα∂∂tFβ ∂tγ satisfy the following conditions:

 (1) The coeﬀicients ηαβ = c1αβ form elements of a constant nondegenerate matrix.
 (2) The quantities cγαβ = η γδ cαβδ represent the structure constants of an associative algebra.
 (3) The function F (t) must be quasi-homogeneous.

    In [16, Chapter 1], Dubrovin formulated a geometric interpretation of the WDVV equation which
is given by the following.

Definition 1.2. A Frobenius algebra A is a finite-dimensional unital, commutative, associative
algebra equipped with an invariant, non-degenerate bilinear pairing η : A ⊗A 7→ C, which is invariant
in the following sense η(A • B, C) = η(A, B • C), ∀A, B, C ∈ A .

    Roughly speaking, Dubrovin–Frobenius manifold is the geometric structure that naturally arise
in the domain of any WDVV solution, which is given by a family of Frobenius algebra on the sheaf
of holomorphic vector fields, a flat structure and some suitable marked vector fields. An impor-

Gromov–Witten potential. Another source of Dubrovin–Frobenius manifolds comes from Landau–
Ginzburg superpotential, which are unfolding of singularities or family of covering over CP1 . In the
analytic theory of Dubrovin–Frobenius manifold, there exist two flat connection. The 1st structure
connection is called Dubrovin connection and it is defined below.

from the multiplication by the Euler vector field (1.5) are diagonal around such a point.

2 Construction of Dubrovin superpotential
The objective of this section is to review the Dubrovin construction of the superpotential associated
with any WDVV solution in [16, Appendix J] and [18, Chapter 5]. Additionally, we will employ this
approach to deduce the Landau–Ginzburg superpotential for the big quantum cohomology of CP2 .
The central element of this construction is the inverse period map of the extended Gauss–Manin
connection (1.10).

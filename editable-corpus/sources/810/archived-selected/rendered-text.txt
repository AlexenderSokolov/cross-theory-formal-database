2.1 (g, K)-module
We recall the notion of (g, K)-modules. Let G be an aﬀine algebraic group over C. A linear action of
G on a vector space V is said to be rational if the action is locally finite and any finite-dimensional
subrepresentation is a representation of an algebraic group. In the case, V is called a G-module. If
G is reductive, any G-module is completely reducible.
   Let us recall the definitions of pairs (g, K) and (g, K)-modules. In this paper, we treat only the
case of connected reductive K. We refer the reader to [16, Chapter I] for the general case.

Definition 2.1. Let g be a finite-dimensional complex Lie algebra and K a connected reductive
algebraic group such that k is a subalgebra of g. We say that (g, K) is a pair if the adjoint action of k
on g lifts to a rational action of K. A pair (h, L) is said to be a subpair of (g, K) if h is a subalgebra
of g and L is a connected reductive subgroup of K.

Definition 2.2. Let (g, K) be a pair and V a g-module. We denote by Vk the sum of all finite-
dimensional k-submodules, and by VK the sum of all k-submodules that lift to K-modules. We say
that V is a (g, K)-module if V = VK .

    Clearly, VK is a (g, K)-module. Since we have assumed that K is connected, the K-action on a
(g, K)-module V is uniquely determined by the g-action.
    Let (g, K) be a pair and V a (g, K)-module. Then V |K is completely reducible and U (g)K acts
naturally on V K . Since k acts on V K trivially, the U (g)K -action factors through (U (g)/U (g)k)K ≃
U (g)K /(U (g)k)K . It is well-known that if V is irreducible, then the U (g)K -module V K is irreducible
or zero. See [6, Theorem 4.2.1].

2.2 Translation functor
Our purpose in this paper is to show the irreducibility of some (g′ ⊕ g, ∆(G′ ))-modules. We will
reduce the problem to the case of enough large infinitesimal characters. To do so, we need the
translation functor. We refer the reader to [16, Chapter VII].
    Let g be a finite-dimensional complex reductive Lie algebra. Fix a Cartan subalgebra t of g
and denote by WG the Weyl group of g. We identify Z(g) with U (t)WG via the Harish-Chandra
isomorphism. Then characters of Z(g) are parametrized by elements in t∗ /WG . When we say that
χ ∈ t∗ /WG is an infinitesimal character, χ is identified with a homomorphism Z(g) → C and Ker(χ)
is a maximal ideal of Z(g). For λ ∈ t∗ , we denote by [λ] the corresponding infinitesimal character
in t∗ /WG , and for a subcategory C of Mod(g), we denote by C[λ] (or Cλ ) the full subcategory of C
consisting of objects with the generalized infinitesimal character [λ].
    For a g-module V and an infinitesimal character χ ∈ t∗ /WG , set

                                 Pχ (V ) := {v ∈ V | Ker(χ)n v = 0, ∃n ∈ N}.

Note that n can depend on v. Then Pχ (V ) is a g-submodule of V , and called the primary component
with the infinitesimal character χ. When V is the direct sum of all primary components, we say that
the direct sum decomposition is the primary decomposition. The following fact is a direct consequence
of [16, Proposition 7.20]. For a g-module V , we say that V is locally finite Z(g)-module if Z(g)v is
finite-dimensional for any v ∈ V .
Fact 2.5. Any locally Z(g)-finite g-module has the primary decomposition.
   We shall recall the translation functor. Let µ ∈ t∗ be an algebraically integral weight of g.
Let F (µ) be a finite-dimensional irreducible g-module with the extreme weight µ. For a g-module V
with an generalized infinitesimal character [λ] ∈ t∗ /WG , set

                                           Tλλ+µ (V ) := P[λ+µ] (F (µ) ⊗ V ).

Then P[λ+µ] (F (µ) ⊗ V ) is a g-module with the generalized infinitesimal character [λ + µ].

is a direct summand of F (µ) ⊗ V . This implies that Tλλ+µ is an exact functor from Mod(g)[λ] to
Mod(g)[λ+µ] . The functor is called the translation functor.

2.3 Discrete decomposability
Our main concern in this paper is discretely decomposable restrictions of g-modules. We recall the
notion of the discrete decomposability. We refer the reader to [20, 24] for the details.
   Let g be a finite-dimensional complex reductive Lie algebra.

Definition 2.8. Let V be a g-module. We say that V is discretely decomposable if V has an
exhaustive g-module filtration 0 = V0 ⊂ V1 ⊂ · · · such that any Vi has finite length. Moreover, if all
Vi are in a subcategory C of Mod(g), we say that V is discretely decomposable in C.

   By definition, a discretely decomposable g-module is locally Z(g)-finite. Hence the following
proposition is a consequence of Fact 2.5.

Proposition 2.9. A discretely decomposable g-module has the primary decomposition.

2.4 Generalized Verma module
This section contains a brief summary of generalized Verma modules. We refer the reader to [10].
For the branching problem part, see [24].
    Let g be a finite-dimensional complex reductive Lie algebra. Fix a semisimple element H ∈ g such
that ad(H) has only real eigenvalues. l(H), u(H) and u(H) denote the sums of eigenspaces of ad(H)
with zero, positive and negative eigenvalues, respectively. Then q(H) := l(H) ⊕ u(H) is a parabolic
subalgebra of g. Similarly, write q(H) := l(H) ⊕ u(H) for the opposite parabolic subalgebra. If H is
clear from the context, we omit ‘(H)’ part as l, u and q.
    Fix a Cartan subalgebra t of l and write WL for the Weyl group of l. Then we have H ∈ t. For a
finite-dimensional completely reducible t-module V , we denote by ∆(V, t) (or ∆(V )) the (multi)set
of all non-zero weights in V . We write ρ(u) for half the sum of all roots in ∆(u, t).
    We shall recall generalized Verma modules. Let F be an l-module. Set

\section{A topological restriction on parahoric subgroups}
\subsection{Statement of the result and idea of the proof}
In this section, we will prove that beside the reductive case, it is impossible to endow $G$ with a structure of topological group for which $K_s$ or $K_I$ are open compact subgroups, where $K_I$ denotes the (standard) Iwahori subgroup.\footnote{We recall that $K_I$ is the fixer in $G$ of the fundamental local chamber $C_0^+$.} In fact, we will prove the following result, which is slightly more general.
\begin{theorem}\label{jep88localTheorem3_1}
Let $F$ be a type $0$ face of $\jepPubBbb{A}$ and let $K_F$ be its fixer in $G$. If $W^v$ is infinite, then there is no topology of topological group on $G$ for which $K_F$ is open and compact.
\end{theorem}
Let $F$ be a type $0$ face of $\jepPubBbb{A}$, i.e., a local face whose vertex lies in $Y$. First note that, up to replacing $F$ by $h\cdot F$ for some well-chosen $h\in G$, which leads to consider the conjugate of $K_F$ under $h$ instead of $K_F$, we can assume that $F$ is contained in $C_0^\pm$. As the treatment of both cases is similar, we assume that $F$ is contained in $C_0^+$. To prove Theorem~\ref{jep88localTheorem3_1}, it is enough to prove the existence of $g\in G$ such that $K_F/(K_F\cap g\cdot K_F.g^{-1})$ is infinite.

To explain the strategy of proof, we need to introduce some more notations. Let $\alpha\in\Phi^+$ and $(w,i)\in W^v\times I$ be such that $\alpha=w\cdot\alpha_i$. For any $k\in\jepPubBbb{Z}$, set
\[
M_k^\alpha:=\{t\in\jepPubBbb{A}\mid\alpha(t)=k\},\qquad D_k^\alpha:=\{t\in\jepPubBbb{A}\mid\alpha(t)\leqslant k\},
\]
and let $K_{\alpha,k}$ be the fixer of $D_k^\alpha$ in $G$. Furthermore, pick a panel $P_k^\alpha$ in $M_k^\alpha$ and a chamber $C_k^\alpha$ contained in $\operatorname{conv}(M_k^\alpha,M_{k+1}^\alpha)$ that dominates $P_k^\alpha$.

For any $i\in I$, we let $q_i+1$ (resp. $q_i'+1$) be the number of chambers containing $P_0^{\alpha_i}$ (resp. $P_1^{\alpha_i}$). By~\cite[Prop.~2.9]{jep88refRou11} and~\cite[Lem.~3.2]{jep88refHeb16}, $q_i$ and $q_i'$ do not depend on the choices of the panels $P_0^{\alpha_i}$ and $P_1^{\alpha_i}$. (This fact will be explained in the proof of Lemma~\ref{jep88localLemma3_4}.) As $\alpha_i(\alpha_i^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee})=2$, and as there exists an element of $G$ that induces on $\jepPubBbb{A}$ a translation of vector $\alpha_i^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}$ (because we assumed that the stabilizer $N$ of $\jepPubBbb{A}$ in $G$ induces $W^v\ltimes Y$ for group of affine automorphisms), the value of $1+q_i$ (resp. $1+q_i'$) is also the number of chambers that contain $P_{2k}^{\alpha_i}$ (resp. $P_{2k+1}^{\alpha_i}$) for any integer $k$.

Let us now explain the basic idea of the proof. Pick $g\in G$ such that $g\cdot0\in C_f^v$ and set $F':=g\cdot F$: then $K_F/(K_F\cap K_{F'})$ is in bijection with $K_F\cdot F'$. For $\alpha=w\cdot\alpha_i$, set $\widetilde{K_\alpha}:=\bigcup_{k\in\jepPubBbb{Z}}K_{\alpha,k}$: then $\jepPubBbb{T}_\alpha:=\widetilde{K_\alpha}\cdot\jepPubBbb{A}$ is a semi-homogeneous extended tree with parameters $q_i$ and $q_i'$. Using the thickness of $\jepPubScript{I}$, we can prove that the number $n_\alpha$ of walls between $0$ and $g\cdot0$ that are parallel to $\alpha^{-1}(\{0\})$ satisfies $|K_{\alpha,1}g\cdot0|\geqslant2^{n_\alpha}$, which implies that $|K_Fg\cdot0|\geqslant2^{n_\alpha}$. As $n_\alpha$ can be made arbitrarily large (for a suitable choice of $\alpha$) when $W^v$ is infinite, this will end the proof.

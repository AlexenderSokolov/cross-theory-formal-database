     Elaborating on our approach, we now present some new consequences for refuters
that might be of independent interest. We use SATn to denote the decision version of the
satisfiability problem for De Morgan Boolean formulas represented by n-bit strings (our
results are robust to encoding details). As in the discussion above, we say that a refuter R
for a language L succeeds against a class C of devices on input length n if, for every E ∈ C,
R(1n , E) outputs x ∈ {0, 1}n such that E(x) ̸= L(x). Observe that the existence of such a
refuter implies that L ∈/ C.

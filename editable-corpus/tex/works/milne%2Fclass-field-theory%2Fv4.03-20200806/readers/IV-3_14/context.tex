\noindent\textsc{Corollary 3.6}\quad Let $A$ be a central simple algebra over $k$. A field $L$ of finite degree over $k$ splits $A$ if and only if there exists an algebra $B$ similar to $A$ containing $L$ and such that
\[
[B:k]=[L:k]^2. \tag{37}
\]
In particular, every subfield $L$ of $A$ of degree $[A:k]^{1/2}$ over $k$ splits $A$.

\noindent\textsc{Proof.}\quad Suppose $L$ splits $A$. Then $L$ also splits $A^{\mathrm{opp}}$, say, $A^{\mathrm{opp}}\otimes_k L=\operatorname{End}_L(V)$. This equality states that $A^{\mathrm{opp}}\otimes_k L$ is the centralizer of $L$ in $\operatorname{End}_k(V)$, and so $L$ is the centralizer of $A^{\mathrm{opp}}\otimes_k L$ in $\operatorname{End}_k(V)$ (see 3.1). Let $B$ be the centralizer of $A^{\mathrm{opp}}$ in $\operatorname{End}_k(V)$. I claim that $B$ satisfies the required conditions. Certainly, $B\supset L$, and Corollary 3.3 shows that $B$ is central simple and that $B\otimes_k A^{\mathrm{opp}}\simeq\operatorname{End}_k(V)$. On tensoring both sides with $A$ and using that $A\otimes_k A^{\mathrm{opp}}$ is a matrix algebra, we find that $B\sim A$.

For the converse, it suffices to show that $L$ splits $B$. Because $L$ is commutative, $L=L^{\mathrm{opp}}\subset B^{\mathrm{opp}}$, and because $[L:k]=\sqrt{[B:k]}$, $L$ is equal to its centralizer in $B^{\mathrm{opp}}$. Therefore the centralizer of $1\otimes L$ in $B\otimes_k B^{\mathrm{opp}}$ is $B\otimes_k L$. When we identify $B\otimes_k B^{\mathrm{opp}}$ with $\operatorname{End}_k(B)$ (endomorphisms of $B$ as a $k$-vector space---see 2.9), the centralizer of $L$ becomes identified with $\operatorname{End}_L(B)$ (endomorphisms as an $L$-vector space). This completes the proof. \hfill$\square$

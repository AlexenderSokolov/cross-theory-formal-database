\noindent\textit{Allowed standard prerequisites (editorial).}\begin{itemize}
\item Filtration as in property (P). An exhaustive increasing filtration of P by dg submodules whose successive quotients are direct sums of shifted copies of A.. It supplies the successive inclusions F\_i P -> F\_\{i+1\} P that the construction of the two maps uses (source lines 2426-2432, 2440-2446).
\item Admissible short exact sequence of dg modules. A short exact sequence of dg modules which splits as graded modules, so that the middle term is the direct sum of the outer terms.. The conclusion is precisely that this sequence is admissible, which is proved by writing down the splitting (source lines 2434-2436, 2446-2448).
\item Splitting of the successive inclusions. For each i the inclusion F\_i P -> F\_\{i+1\} P admits a graded left inverse s\_i.. The composed maps s\_\{j,i\} and the tuple formula for the left inverse are built from those s\_i (source lines 2444-2448).
\end{itemize}\medskip
\begin{lemma}
\label{lemma-property-P-sequence}
Let $(A, \text{d})$ be a differential graded algebra.
Let $P$ be a differential graded $A$-module. If $F_\bullet$
is a filtration as in property (P), then we obtain an
admissible short exact sequence
$$
0 \to
\bigoplus\nolimits F_iP \to
\bigoplus\nolimits F_iP \to P \to 0
$$
of differential graded $A$-modules.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The second map is the direct sum of the inclusion maps.
The first map on the summand $F_iP$ of the source is the sum
of the identity $F_iP \to F_iP$ and the negative of the inclusion
map $F_iP \to F_{i + 1}P$. Choose homomorphisms $s_i : F_{i + 1}P \to F_iP$
of graded $A$-modules which are left inverse to the inclusion maps.
Composing gives maps $s_{j, i} : F_jP \to F_iP$ for all $j > i$.
Then a left inverse of the first arrow maps $x \in F_jP$ to
$(s_{j, 0}(x), s_{j, 1}(x), \ldots, s_{j, j - 1}(x), 0, \ldots)$
in $\bigoplus F_iP$.
\end{proof}

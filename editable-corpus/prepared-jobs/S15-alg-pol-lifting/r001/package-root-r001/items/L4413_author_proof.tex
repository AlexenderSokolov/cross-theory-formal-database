\noindent\textit{Allowed standard prerequisites (editorial).}\begin{itemize}
\item Action of a finite group on a ring map and the invariant subrings A\textasciicircum{}G and B\textasciicircum{}G. the hypotheses at statement lines 32541-32542. Makes sense of the requirement that P lie in A\textasciicircum{}G[T] and that its image lie in B\textasciicircum{}G[T].
\item Multiplicativity of a ring map on products and the fact that an invariant element is fixed by every group element. used at proof line 32549. Is what lets the image of prod (T - sigma(a)) be identified with prod (T - b) = (T - b)\textasciicircum{}n.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lemma-pol-lifting}
Let $\varphi : A \to B$ be a surjection of rings. Let $G$ be a finite group
of order $n$ acting on $\varphi : A \to B$. If $b \in B^G$, then
there exists a monic polynomial $P \in A^G[T]$ which maps to
$(T - b)^n$ in $B^G[T]$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose $a \in A$ lifting $b$ and set
$P = \prod_{\sigma \in G} (T - \sigma(a))$.
\end{proof}

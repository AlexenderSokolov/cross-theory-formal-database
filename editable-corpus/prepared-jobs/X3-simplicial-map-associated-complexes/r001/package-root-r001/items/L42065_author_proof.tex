\noindent\textit{Editorial scope.} One original result of the Stacks Project's Simplicial Methods chapter, counted once: the canonical maps N(U\_n) to U\_n define a morphism of complexes N(U) to s(U).\par\medskip
\noindent\textit{Allowed standard prerequisites (editorial).}\begin{itemize}
\item Normalised and associated complexes. For a simplicial object U, the associated chain complex s(U) with s(U)\_n = U\_n and differential the alternating sum of the face maps, and the normalised complex N(U) whose n-th term is the quotient of U\_n by the degenerate simplices.. They are the source and target of the canonical map (source lines 3999-4001).
\item Vanishing of the faces on the normalised subobject. For i smaller than n the face map d\textasciicircum{}n\_i vanishes on the kernel of the sum of the degeneracy maps, i.e. on the normalised subobject.. It kills all but the last term of the alternating sum (source lines 4006-4008).
\item The boundary map of the normalised complex. The differential of N(U) is induced by the last face map d\textasciicircum{}n\_n, up to the standard sign.. It identifies the surviving term with the boundary map (source lines 4008-4009).
\end{itemize}\medskip
\begin{lemma}
\label{lemma-map-associated-complexes}
Let $\mathcal{A}$ be an abelian category.
Let $U$ be a simplicial object of $\mathcal{A}$.
The canonical map $N(U_n) \to U_n$ gives rise to
a morphism of complexes $N(U) \to s(U)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
This is clear because the differential
on $s(U)_n = U_n$ is $\sum (-1)^i d^n_i$ and
the maps $d^n_i$, $i < n$ are zero on $N(U_n)$,
whereas the restriction of $(-1)^nd^n_n$ is the boundary
map of $N(U)$ by definition.
\end{proof}

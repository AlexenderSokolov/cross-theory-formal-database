\noindent\textit{Editorial scope.} One original result of the Stacks Project's Differential Graded Algebra chapter, counted once: the rotated triangle of an admissible short exact sequence is isomorphic to the cone of its connecting morphism.\par\medskip
\noindent\textit{Allowed standard prerequisites (editorial).}\begin{itemize}
\item Cone of a morphism of dg modules. The cone C(f) of a morphism f : X -> Y is the dg module Y direct sum X[1] with differential given by the block matrix of d\_Y and the shifted d\_X against f.. It gives the differential matrix of C(delta[-1]) that is compared with d\_B (source lines 1006-1012).
\item Associated triangle of an admissible short exact sequence. An admissible short exact sequence 0 -> K -> L -> M -> 0 of dg modules determines a morphism delta : M -> K[1] and a triangle (K, L, M, alpha, beta, delta).. It supplies the connecting morphism delta whose shift enters the matrix of d\_B (source lines 1003-1006).
\item Splitting of an admissible sequence. An admissible short exact sequence of dg modules splits as graded modules, so the middle term can be written as the direct sum of the outer terms.. The proof chooses splittings to write L = K direct sum M and to identify alpha and beta with the inclusion and projection (source lines 998-1000).
\end{itemize}\medskip
\begin{lemma}
\label{lemma-rotate-triangle}
Let $(A, \text{d})$ be a differential graded algebra.
Let $\alpha : K \to L$ and $\beta : L \to M$
define an admissible short exact sequence
$$
0 \to K \to L \to M \to 0
$$
of differential graded $A$-modules.
Let $(K, L, M, \alpha, \beta, \delta)$
be the associated triangle. Then the triangles
$$
(M[-1], K, L, \delta[-1], \alpha, \beta)
\quad\text{and}\quad
(M[-1], K, C(\delta[-1]), \delta[-1], i, p)
$$
are isomorphic.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Using a choice of splittings we write $L = K \oplus M$ and we identify
$\alpha$ and $\beta$ with the natural inclusion and projection maps.
By construction of $\delta$ we have
$$
d_B =
\left(
\begin{matrix}
d_K & \delta \\
0 & d_M
\end{matrix}
\right)
$$
On the other hand the cone of $\delta[-1] : M[-1] \to K$
is given as $C(\delta[-1]) = K \oplus M$ with differential identical
with the matrix above! Whence the lemma.
\end{proof}

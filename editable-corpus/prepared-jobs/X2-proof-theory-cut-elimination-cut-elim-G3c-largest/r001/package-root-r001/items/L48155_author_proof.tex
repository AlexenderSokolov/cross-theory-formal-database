\noindent\textit{Allowed standard prerequisites (editorial).}\begin{itemize}
\item Cut rank. The rank of a cut is the size of the cut formula; the maximal rank of a proof is the largest rank of its cuts.. The proof stratifies cuts by rank d and counts n the cuts of maximal rank (754-756).
\item Maximal cut reduction. A topmost cut of maximal rank in a G3c proof can be replaced so that the end-sequent is unchanged and all cuts in the replaced part have strictly smaller rank; the unit proves this as its own lemma.. It performs the replacement step in the inner induction (758-762).
\item Topmost cut. A cut is topmost when no cut of the same or greater rank occurs above it in the proof tree.. Picking a topmost cut of rank d ensures the replacement does not disturb other maximal cuts above it (758-759).
\end{itemize}\medskip

\noindent\textit{Source reference.} Original lemma lem-max-cut-red-G3c: sources/olp-proof-theory-cut-elimination/pinned-original-source.tex, source lines 528--528. The author proof delegates the replacement of a topmost maximal cut to the unit's reduction lemma. The load-bearing work of that replacement is carried by the separately proved lemma of the same unit; the counted proof retains its own double induction in full.\par\medskip
\begin{thm}\ollabel{thm:cut-elim-G3c-largest}
Any !!{proof} $\pi$ in $\Log{G3c} + \CutCS$ can be transformed into a proof in~\Log{G3c}.
\end{thm}

\begin{proof}
  First we prove that all cuts of maximal rank in~$\pi$ can be
  removed. Let $d$ be the maximal rank of cuts occuring in~$\pi$, and
  let $n$ be the number of cuts of rank~$d$. The proof is by induction
  on~$n$. If $n=0$ there is nothing to prove. If $n>0$, pick a topmost
  cut of rank~$d$, and let $\pi_1$ be the sub-!!{proof} ending in it.
  By \olref{lem:max-cut-red-G3c}, there is !!a{proof}~$\pi_1'$ of the
  same end-sequent with all cuts of rank $<d$. Replace $\pi_1$ by
  $\pi_1'$ in~$\pi$. This results in !!a{proof} containing $n-1$ cuts
  of rank~$d$, and the induction hypothesis applies.

  Now the result follows by induction on~$d$. If $d=0$, all cuts are
  atomic, and by the previous result can all be removed. If $d>0$,
  the previous result yields a proof in which all cuts of rank $d$
  have been replaced by cuts of rank~$<d$. Since $d$ was the maximum
  rank of cuts in~$\pi$, we have proof in which the maximal rank of
  cuts is $<d$, and the induction hypothesis applies.
\end{proof}
